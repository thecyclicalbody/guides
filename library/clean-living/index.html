Clean living

Some substances in plastics, cosmetics and pesticides can act like hormones in the body. You cannot avoid them completely, but you can lower your daily exposure with a few simple swaps. Change one thing at a time, when a product runs out.

In the kitchen

  • Store food in glass or stainless steel, and never heat food in plastic.
  • Replace non-stick pans with stainless steel, cast iron or ceramic.
  • Choose organic for the fruit and vegetables with the most pesticide residue (the Dirty Dozen, such as strawberries, spinach, apples and grapes).
  • Use a water filter for drinking water.
  • Avoid plastic-lined cans, plastic bags and touching receipts.
  • Limit large predatory fish such as swordfish, shark and king mackerel.

In the bathroom

  • Switch to organic cotton or reusable menstrual products.
  • Choose skincare without parabens, sulfates and synthetic fragrance, and learn to read labels for phthalates, triclosan and formaldehyde.
  • A shower filter is an optional extra.

In the house

  • Use non-toxic cleaning products or vinegar-based cleaners.
  • Choose fragrance-free or naturally scented laundry detergent, and skip air fresheners and scented candles.
  • Air your home every day.
  • Wear natural fabrics such as cotton and linen where you can.
