Meals and blood sugar

Protein

Protein provides the amino acids your body uses to build tissue, make neurotransmitters and clear hormones through the liver. It also keeps your blood sugar steady and makes you feel full for longer. Most women eat less protein than they need, especially at breakfast.

How much. Aim for about 1.2 to 1.6 g of protein per kg of body weight per day, spread over your meals. For someone of 65 kg that is roughly 80 to 105 g a day. Breakfast is the meal where most women fall short, so aim for at least 30 g there.

The amounts are approximate and differ per brand; check the label if you want to be precise.

Smaller protein additions: hemp seeds (3 tbsp, about 10 g), pumpkin seeds (2 tbsp, about 8 g), edamame (100 g, about 11 g), chickpeas (100 g cooked, about 9 g), collagen powder in tea (10 to 15 g).

Snacks. Always combine protein and fat, for example a boiled egg with olives, Greek yogurt with walnuts, cheese with cucumber, or edamame with tahini.

Plant-based tip. Combine legumes with grains (lentils with rice, hummus with bread) and rotate between tofu, tempeh, legumes, seeds and pea protein. Hummus alone is a side, not a main protein source.
