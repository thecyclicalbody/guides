Stress, rhythm and sleep

Stress and your cycle

Your stress system and your cycle are run from the same part of the brain. When your body senses ongoing stress, it gives priority to getting through the day, and ovulation and progesterone production come second. Stress here is broader than a busy agenda: skipped meals, blood sugar dips, too little sleep, intense training and dehydration all count as stress for your body.

Take away physical stress first

  • Eat enough, with protein and fat at every meal, and eat within an hour of waking (see Breakfast and blood sugar).
  • Drink enough, with a pinch of salt or an electrolyte sachet (see Hydration and minerals).
  • Walk about 60 minutes a day in total, and move after breakfast rather than before it.
  • Limit very intense workouts for now (see Movement and strength training).

Calm your nervous system

  • Breathe slowly into your belly for 3 to 5 minutes, twice a day.
  • Take a few slow breaths before each meal.
  • Write things down: a few pages in the morning, or 10 minutes of worry time at a fixed moment.
  • Notice what stress feels like in your body (a tight jaw, shallow breathing, a racing mind) and name it. Some people calm down best by sitting still, others by moving, shaking or dancing. Find out what works for you.

Make room

  • Plan weekly time for something you enjoy, even if it is 10 minutes.
  • Spend time in nature and with people who make you laugh.
  • Notice where you say yes when you mean no, and practice small boundaries.
  • Limit social media, especially first thing in the morning and before bed.

If you like data, a wearable that measures heart rate variability (HRV), such as an Oura ring or Garmin, can show how your body responds to stress and recovery.
