Soaking, sprouting and fermenting

Grains, legumes, nuts and seeds contain substances such as phytic acid that can make them harder to digest and reduce how well you absorb minerals like iron and zinc. Traditional preparation lowers these substances.

  • Soaking. Cover dried legumes or whole grains with plenty of water and soak them for 8 to 24 hours, depending on the type. Rinse well and cook in fresh water. A splash of apple cider vinegar or lemon juice in the soaking water helps for grains.
  • Sprouting. After soaking, leave legumes or seeds in a jar or sieve, rinsing twice a day, until small sprouts appear after 1 to 3 days. Cook sprouted legumes before eating.
  • Fermenting. Sourdough bread, tempeh, miso and fermented vegetables are examples of fermentation. Choose real sourdough bread over regular bread where you can.
  • Nuts and seeds. Soak raw nuts for a few hours, rinse, and dry them in a low oven if you want them crunchy.
