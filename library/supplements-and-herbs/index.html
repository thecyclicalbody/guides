How we work with supplements and herbs

Food and daily habits are the foundation of your plan. Supplements and herbs come on top of that: they can fill nutrient gaps faster and give specific pathways in your body extra support, but they do not replace a good diet. That is why we only add them when they fit the phase you are in, and why your personal plan lists exactly which ones, how much, when and for how long.

How we work with them

  • We start with a small number and build up step by step. That way we can see what helps and what you react to.
  • Every supplement in your plan comes with its purpose, dose, timing, duration and where to buy a good-quality version.
  • These are suggestions, not prescriptions. If you use medication, are pregnant, breastfeeding or trying to conceive, we check every supplement and herb for interactions first, and where needed together with your doctor.
  • Some nutrients, such as vitamin D and iron, are only supplemented after your levels have been tested, because your body stores them.
  • Herbs are powerful. Many are not suitable during pregnancy or breastfeeding, and some interact with medication such as blood thinners, the pill or antidepressants. Never add herbs on your own without discussing it first.

Why quality matters

Supplements differ a lot in quality. Cheap products often contain forms your body absorbs poorly, or fillers, sugars and dyes. A good supplement uses well-absorbed forms (for example magnesium glycinate instead of magnesium oxide, or methylated B vitamins), contains no unnecessary additives and is preferably tested by an independent lab.

Taking them

  • Take supplements with a meal unless your plan says otherwise; that improves absorption and is gentler on your stomach.
  • Note anything you notice in the first weeks, positive or negative, in your tracking notes.
  • Stop and contact me if you experience side effects such as nausea, headaches, skin reactions or changes in your cycle that worry you.
