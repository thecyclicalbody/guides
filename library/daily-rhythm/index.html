Stress, rhythm and sleep

Your daily rhythm: light, mornings and sleep

Almost every hormone in your body follows a 24-hour rhythm, set by an internal clock in your brain. That clock is set mainly by light, and also by when you eat, move and sleep. In the first 30 to 45 minutes after waking your cortisol rises sharply (the cortisol awakening response, or CAR) to get you going, and in the evening melatonin takes over so you can sleep. A steady rhythm supports this curve, which in turn supports blood sugar, sleep, stress resilience and, over time, ovulation and a more regular cycle.

The anchor: regular times

  • Go to bed and get up at the same time every day, also at weekends. Aim for 8 to 9 hours of sleep, at a regular length rather than catching up at the weekend.

Your morning

  • Get up when your alarm goes, without snoozing.
  • Get daylight as soon as you can: open the curtains, step outside, or walk for a few minutes without sunglasses. Even on a cloudy day, outdoor light is many times brighter than indoor light. In the dark months a daylight lamp of 10,000 lux during breakfast can help.
  • Drink a glass of water or herbal tea within 30 minutes of waking.
  • Leave your phone and email for the first part of the morning.
  • Eat breakfast within an hour of waking (see Breakfast and blood sugar).
  • Gentle movement in the morning, such as a short walk or stretch, ideally outdoors.

During the day

  • Spend time outside every day, at least 10 minutes, and more around midday if you can. Short moments add up: step outside for about 5 minutes every hour. A strong day signal helps your body produce melatonin more easily in the evening, which supports your sleep.
  • Keep regular meal times, with your larger meals earlier in the day and dinner early in the evening.
  • If you train hard, do it mid-morning or early afternoon rather than in the evening.

Your evening

  • Start winding down an hour before bed: dim the lights, switch to warm or orange light, and put screens away 1 to 2 hours before sleep, or use blue light glasses if that is not possible.
  • Finish your last meal 2 to 3 hours before bed, and stop alcohol well before sleep.
  • If your head is busy, write your worries down in another room and leave the notebook there.
  • Relaxing options: a warm bath or shower, a body scan, yoga nidra or a short meditation.

Your bedroom

  • Keep it dark and cool, around 15 to 18 °C.
  • Keep your phone out of the bedroom and use an analog alarm clock.
  • Make it a place that feels comfortable and safe to you.
  • If you get up at night, use as little light as possible.
