Your cycle

Tracking your cycle

Two signs show what your hormones are doing in real time: cervical fluid shows rising estrogen before ovulation, and basal body temperature confirms that ovulation has happened. Together they tell us more than the length of your cycle alone.

Cervical fluid. Check each time you use the bathroom by wiping front to back, and note the texture once a day.

What you noticeWhat it means
Dry or stickyLow estrogen, early or late in the cycle
Creamy, lotion-likeEstrogen is rising
Clear, slippery, stretchy like raw egg whitePeak fertility, ovulation is approaching
Dry againOvulation has passed and progesterone is rising

Basal body temperature (BBT). Measure every morning right after waking, before getting up, at about the same time. Use a thermometer with two decimals, or a device such as Tempdrop.

  • Before ovulation your temperature is lower, typically around 36.1 to 36.4 °C.
  • After ovulation, progesterone raises it by about 0.2 to 0.5 °C. A rise that lasts at least three days confirms ovulation.
  • A temperature that stays flat all cycle can mean ovulation did not happen.

How to use it. Note both signs daily in an app such as Read Your Body or in a notebook. We look at the patterns over two to three cycles together, so you do not need to interpret them on your own.
