Meals and blood sugar

Building your plateA balanced plate keeps your blood sugar steady, keeps you full for longer and gives your body the building blocks it needs to make hormones.Eating orderThe order in which you eat the parts of your meal changes how fast sugar enters your blood.ProteinProtein provides the amino acids your body uses to build tissue, make neurotransmitters and clear hormones through the liver.Breakfast and blood sugarIn the first hour after waking, your body releases a peak of cortisol to get you going.Hunger awarenessThis exercise helps you notice the difference between physical hunger and eating for other reasons, such as stress, boredom or habit.

Digestion, liver and lymph

Fiber and digestionFiber feeds your gut bacteria, keeps your bowel movements regular and binds used hormones in your gut so they leave your body instead of being reabsorbed.Hydration and mineralsYour body needs water and minerals for digestion, energy and a regular bowel movement.Soaking, sprouting and fermentingGrains, legumes, nuts and seeds contain substances such as phytic acid that can make them harder to digest and reduce how well you absorb minerals like iron and zinc.Foods that support your liverYour liver processes used hormones so they can leave your body through your gut and kidneys.Castor oil packsA castor oil pack is a simple evening ritual that many women find calming, and it is traditionally used to support the liver and digestion.Keeping your lymph movingYour lymphatic system is a network of vessels and nodes that collects fluid, waste products and immune cells from your tissues and returns them to your blood.
