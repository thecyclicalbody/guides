Fiber and digestion

Fiber feeds your gut bacteria, keeps your bowel movements regular and binds used hormones in your gut so they leave your body instead of being reabsorbed. Aim for about 30 g a day, and build up slowly over two to three weeks while drinking more water, to avoid bloating.

  • Soluble fiber (oats, chia seeds, ground flaxseed, citrus, berries, kiwi, legumes) forms a gel that slows sugar uptake and softens stool. Apples and pears are also good sources, but their natural sugars (fructose and sorbitol) give some people gas and bloating. If that is you, swap them for berries, citrus or kiwi for a while.
  • Insoluble fiber (vegetables, whole grains, nuts and seeds) adds bulk and keeps things moving.
  • Prebiotic foods feed your good bacteria: onions, leeks, garlic, asparagus, artichoke, oats, legumes, slightly green bananas, and cooked and cooled rice or potatoes. Build these up slowly if they give you gas.
  • Fermented foods such as sauerkraut, kimchi, kefir and pickled vegetables bring in bacteria. Start with a spoonful with your meal, after your vegetables, and build up to 2 to 4 tablespoons a day. If you react to histamine, wait with these until we have discussed it.
  • Broth two to three times a week is gentle on the gut and provides minerals.

What a day with about 30 g of fiber can look like

  • Breakfast: 40 g oats with 1 tablespoon chia seeds and 100 g raspberries (about 14 g)
  • Morning snack: two green kiwis and a small handful of almonds (about 8 g)
  • Lunch: a large salad with 100 g chickpeas and a slice of whole grain bread (about 10 g)
  • Dinner: salmon with 150 g broccoli and a sweet potato (about 9 g)

That adds up to more than 30 g. If you eat around 15 to 20 g on most days now, build up to this over two to three weeks, one extra portion every few days, and drink a little more as you go.

How you eat matters too. Sit down, take a few slow breaths, put your phone away and chew each bite at least 30 times. Digestion starts in your mouth, and a calm state lets your body produce the stomach acid and enzymes it needs.
