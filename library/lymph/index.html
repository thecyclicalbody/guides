Digestion, liver and lymph

Keeping your lymph moving

Your lymphatic system is a network of vessels and nodes that collects fluid, waste products and immune cells from your tissues and returns them to your blood. Unlike your blood, lymph has no heart to pump it. It moves mainly because your muscles squeeze the vessels when you move, and because your diaphragm creates pressure changes when you breathe deeply.

  • Walk every day. Every step contracts your calf and leg muscles, which act as a pump for lymph and blood in your legs.
  • Breathe into your belly. Slow, deep belly breathing moves your diaphragm and helps lymph flow from your belly and chest back to your blood.
  • Move when you sit or stand still. Do 15 calf raises, circle your ankles or walk for a few minutes every hour.
  • Legs up the wall. Lie on your back with your legs resting up against a wall for 5 to 10 minutes. Skip this with glaucoma, heart failure or late pregnancy.
  • Self-massage or dry brushing. Use a soft brush or your hands and make gentle, slow strokes toward your heart. A few minutes before showering is enough. Avoid irritated or broken skin.
  • Drink enough throughout the day (see Hydration and minerals).

Swelling in one arm or leg, or swelling that does not go away, is something to discuss with your doctor rather than treat yourself.
